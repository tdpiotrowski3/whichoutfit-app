What to Wear on a First Date

The goal is not to look impressive. It is to look like yourself on a good day, and to not think about your clothes once you have arrived.

The one rule

Wear something you have already had a good day in. A first date is the worst possible venue for a garment you are still negotiating with — anything you have to adjust, tug or think about will take attention you need elsewhere.

On this page

  1. Match the venue, then add one degree
  2. Fit does more work than anything you can buy
  3. Pick one thing to be interesting
  4. The things that quietly undo a good outfit
  5. Dress for the second half of the evening

Match the venue, then add one degree

The venue sets the floor and you add a single step of effort above it. That step is what reads as interest without reading as strain.

  • Coffee, daytime. Good denim, a well-fitting top or knit, clean shoes. The upgrade is a layer with structure — an overshirt, an unlined blazer, a good jacket.
  • Dinner. Trousers or a midi with a considered top. The upgrade is fabric: something with a bit of drape or sheen reads as evening in a way that cotton does not.
  • Drinks. The most forgiving of the three. Dark denim or trousers, a top with some texture, a boot or a clean shoe. The upgrade is one deliberate accessory.
  • Something active — a walk, a gallery, a market. Dress for the activity honestly and put the effort into fit and condition rather than formality.

Fit does more work than anything you can buy

A well-fitting inexpensive shirt outperforms an expensive one that pulls across the shoulders, every time. If you own one thing that fits genuinely well, build the outfit around it and let everything else be quiet. Shoulder seams sitting where your shoulders end, trousers breaking cleanly, sleeves ending at the wrist bone — these are the details people register without being able to name them.

Pick one thing to be interesting

One point of interest, and the rest supporting it. A strong jacket, an unusual colour, a piece of jewellery with some weight, boots with character. Two competing focal points cancel each other out and three read as costume. This is also the single easiest way to look intentional — it gives someone something specific to comment on, which is a genuinely useful thing on a first date.

The things that quietly undo a good outfit

  • Scuffed or dirty shoes. The most-noticed and least-considered item in any outfit.
  • Visible wrinkles from the commute. Hang it the night before, or choose a fabric that travels.
  • Too much fragrance. In a small booth this is the only thing anyone will remember.
  • A brand-new outfit head to toe. It reads as effort rather than as taste, and you will not know how any of it behaves after two hours.

Dress for the second half of the evening

Restaurants run cold, patios run colder after sunset, and a walk afterwards is a real possibility. One layer you can remove and carry without it becoming a burden covers all three. Check the evening temperature rather than the afternoon one — the difference is frequently ten degrees or more, and being visibly cold is a distraction for both of you.

Common questions

Is it better to be overdressed or underdressed on a first date?

Slightly overdressed, but only slightly. One degree above the venue reads as interest. Two or more reads as misjudging the occasion, which is its own kind of awkward.

Should I wear something new?

Generally no. New clothes are unknown quantities — you do not yet know how they sit after two hours, whether they wrinkle, or whether the shoes will hurt. Wear something proven and save the new piece for a lower-stakes outing.

How much does what I wear actually matter?

Less than the internet suggests, but not nothing. Its real job is to stop you thinking about yourself, so your attention is available for the conversation. An outfit that does that has done its whole job.
